# Code of Conduct

## The standard

Be respectful and constructive. Disagreements about technical approach are
welcome; personal attacks are not.

That sentence is the whole rule. The rest of this document exists so that
"what happens if someone ignores it" has an answer written down before it
is needed, rather than being improvised under pressure.

## What this looks like in practice

This is a repository about getting quantum algorithms *right*, and the
review culture that requires is direct: constructions get challenged,
derivations get checked against theory, and "this is wrong, here is the
`Statevector` comparison that shows it" is a normal and welcome thing to
say. Being blunt about a circuit is not rudeness.

The line is between the work and the person. Criticism of an
implementation, a derivation, or an argument is the point of the project.
Criticism of someone's intelligence, background, or right to be here is
not, and neither is sustained hostility dressed up as technical candor.

Specifically unacceptable:

- Harassment, intimidation, or personal attacks, publicly or privately.
- Demeaning comments about anyone's identity or characteristics.
- Publishing others' private information without their permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions or exhausting reviewers in bad faith.

## Scope

This applies in every space the project occupies — issues, pull requests,
discussions, commit messages, and code review — and to conduct elsewhere
that is directed at project participants because of their involvement here.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to **sean@arboreng.com**. Reports are handled privately,
and you will get a response.

This is a volunteer-maintained project with a single maintainer, so there
is no committee and no appeals board. The maintainer will investigate, and
may respond with anything from a private correction to removing content,
closing a thread, or blocking an account, depending on severity and
whether the behavior continues. Where a report concerns the maintainer,
say so plainly in the report — being the person who wrote the rules does
not exempt anyone from them.

Enforcement decisions favor the people trying to do good work over the
people making that harder.
